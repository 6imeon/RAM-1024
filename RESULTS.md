## 2. How many rounds can each attack break?

|  | SHA-256 | SHA3-512 | BLAKE2b | RAM-1024 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Total rounds | 64 | 24 | 12 | 64 |
| Full avalanche (SAC) at round | 23 | 4 | 2 | 2 |
| Cube attack (≤2^18) breaks to round | none tested ≥ 22 | 4 | 1 | 1 |
| Neural-network (AI) attack breaks to round | 22 | 3 | 1 | 1 |
| Rotational attack breaks to round | none | 2 | none | none |
| **Max round broken in our lab** | **22** | **4** | **1** | **1** |
| Unbroken rounds remaining | 42 | 20 | 11 | 63 |
| Best *published* attack reaches round | 39 | 6 | 8.5 | — (new; assume 8.5) |

Published attacks: **SHA-256** — practical semi-free-start collision 39/64 steps (Li-Liu-Wang 2024); 31-step collision at 2^65.5 (Mendel-Nad-Schläffer 2013); **SHA3-512** — practical collisions on 5-round SHA-3 and 6-round Keccak instances (Guo et al., JoC 2019); SHA3-512 attacks reach 4 rounds; **BLAKE2b** — 8.5-round boomerang distinguisher at 2^474 (Hao 2014); 7.5-round compression pseudo-preimage (Espitau-Fouque-Karpman 2015).

### Measured calibration of the pessimistic model

Expert multiplier = best published reach ÷ our lab's reach. BLAKE2b measured at half-round, RAM-1024 at step resolution:

|  | Lab reach (rounds) | Published reach | Expert multiplier |
|---|---|---|---|
| SHA-256 | 22 | 39 | 1.77× |
| SHA3-512 | 4 | 6 | 1.50× |
| BLAKE2b | 1.5 | 8.5 | 5.67× |
| RAM-1024 | 1.33 | — | × 5.67 (worst, from BLAKE2b) → **7.56 rounds** |

RAM-1024 margin under the worst observed multiplier: 64 ÷ 7.56 = **8.5×** (SHA-3 4.0×, SHA-256 1.6×, BLAKE2b 1.4×).

### Robustness of the calibration

Break boundaries re-measured with 3 fresh seeds (1M-pair neural attacker): stable — BLAKE2b breaks at half-round 3 and resists from 4, RAM-1024 breaks at step 4 and resists from 5, every time.

Each pessimistic model applied *symmetrically* to all four designs (margin; "broken" = model predicts attacks reach every round):

| Model | SHA-256 | SHA3-512 | BLAKE2b | RAM-1024 | RAM leads? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Published reach (standards); measured multiplier × lab reach (RAM) | 1.64× | 4.00× | 1.41× | 8.47× | yes |
| Worst measured multiplier × lab reach, for every design | **broken** | 1.06× | 1.41× | 8.47× | yes |
| Coarse BLAKE2b multiplier (8.5×) × lab reach, for every design | **broken** | **broken** | **broken** | 5.65× | yes |
| Stress: worst multiplier × (lab reach + 1 round), for every design | **broken** | **broken** | **broken** | 4.84× | yes |

**Earlier, assumption-based model.** Years of expert work pushed the standards far beyond what our lab finds (BLAKE2b, which shares RAM's G function: lab 1 → published 8.5 rounds). We assume RAM-1024 falls just as far — 8.5 rounds — which leaves 55 of 64 rounds (8.5×) untouched, vs. SHA-3's 4.0×, SHA-256's 1.6× and BLAKE2b's 1.4×.

## 6. What these results do and do not show

* RAM-1024 diffuses as fast as the best standard, resists every attack in our lab beyond round 1 of 64, and leads every security metric above — including under a deliberately pessimistic model of future cryptanalysis.
* They do **not** prove RAM-1024 secure; no test can. Only years of public cryptanalysis build that confidence, which the standards here have had for 10–25 years. Hence `ramhash`'s default `ram-1024+sha3-512`: secure if *either* survives.
* No public evidence supports "AI is close to breaking SHA-256": our neural attacker stalls at round 22 of 64 while human cryptanalysts reach 39.
